# General Medicine clinic discovery and pilot observation

Use this with the prospective Kerala clinic before setting a build scope or time-saving target. The clinic has not yet agreed to participate. Discuss workflows without copying identifiable patient records; obtain the clinic's permission and an appropriate patient process before observing or using real cases.

## 30-minute doctor conversation

### 1. A recent complex visit (10 minutes)

Ask the doctor to describe one recent General Medicine consultation involving a thick file of prior records, without sharing patient identifiers.

1. What brought the patient in, about how many pages did they bring, and which document types were present?
2. Which pages did you look for first? Which facts affected today's consultation or note?
3. How long did finding and reading those records take? How long did transferring facts into today's note take?
4. What did you skip because of time? What mistakes or omissions would have mattered most?
5. How often do visits like this occur in a typical clinic day or week? Which patients would actually benefit from a record brief?

### 2. Intake reality (8 minutes)

1. When does the patient hand over the file? What is the usual time from check-in to seeing the doctor?
2. Could a patient photograph pages while waiting? When would a nurse need to help, and how much time could that nurse realistically spend?
3. If the bundle is too large, which document categories should be captured first? How should the doctor be told that pages remain uncaptured?
4. Who checks that uploaded pages belong to this patient? What happens when an image is blurry or a page is missing?
5. Does the clinic have reliable mobile data or Wi-Fi at the intake area? Are there existing scanners or clinic devices?

### 3. Doctor view and safety (7 minutes)

1. What must appear in a 30-second brief for this kind of visit? Which information belongs in the longer timeline?
2. Would the doctor open the original page for every important medication, allergy, diagnosis, or result? Which ambiguities must be highlighted?
3. How should an uncertain handwritten medicine name or dose appear? Who can resolve it during the visit?
4. How should the screen show that more pages arrived after the doctor first opened the brief?
5. What clinically important error or omission would make you stop using the brief?

### 4. Current record systems and follow-up (5 minutes)

1. What software currently holds the clinic's consultation notes and prescriptions? Record product/vendor, whether it is web or local, and who can contact the vendor about interfaces.
2. Do staff use ABHA, ABDM record sharing, Kerala eHealth, or another patient identifier today? Ask them to show the exact workflow if they do; do not infer use from a logo or registration.
3. Can the current system export or import a note or patient record? Which formats are actually available in this clinic?
4. Would the doctor review sample briefs and help set a minimum useful time saving and an acceptable error standard?

## Decision after observation

Compare similar complex visits under the existing process and the prototype. Count doctor review and documentation time together; also report patient and staff capture time separately. Ask the doctor to define the minimum useful doctor-time saving and the maximum acceptable clinically important errors before reviewing prototype results. A workflow that simply shifts a large task onto nurses needs redesign. If the clinic lacks a usable EHR interface, the pilot can still show a doctor-facing brief and support doctor-approved copying or export while interoperability is investigated.

## Follow-up items for the clinic and EHR vendor

- Exact EHR/practice-management product and version, vendor contact, hosting arrangement, and available API or export documentation.
- Whether the clinic is participating in ABDM, and in which role; whether any ABHA or Kerala eHealth workflow is used in routine visits.
- Who controls patient registration and record matching, and what identifier can be used for a visit-specific upload link.
- Which patient-consent, access, retention, and correction processes the clinic already uses.
- Whether the vendor permits a test environment and sample records that are safe to use for interface development.
